# Reactivation list — the four existing reviewers

**Prepared 2026-10-21. Every word below is a DRAFT for Logan to hand the owner.**

**I contacted nobody.** No email, no call, no text, no DM, no reply to any review. No
profile was touched. These are scripts to be read, judged, and spoken by Charles Mills —
or not spoken at all, if he knows something about the customer that I don't. **He
outranks this document in every case.**

---

## Why these four, before anybody else

Mills has served hundreds of customers since 1979. These four are the ones who have
**already publicly endorsed the business** — they clicked five stars with their real
names attached. That makes them, by a distance, the highest-probability conversations in
the entire 90-day plan, which is why `growth/REVIEW-ASK-KIT.md` puts them in Days 8–14
ahead of everything else.

**But two of them must not be asked for a review**, and this is the part that is easy to
get wrong. Jose Oliveira and Candis West have already written one. A person can only
hold one review per business — asking them for "a review" invites them to delete and
repost, and a deleted review is a permanently lost review if the new one never lands.
**So they get asked for something else entirely.**

### Source and confidence

All four come from **CLAUDE.md** (checked 2026-10-21), which records them from a Google
Business Profile screenshot. Corroborated at the aggregate level only: the count of 4 and
the 5.0 average appear on [Yahoo Local](https://local.yahoo.com/info-10214240-mills-electric-brockton/)
("4 reviews", "5.0" — fetched 2026-10-21) and on the
[Yelp listing](https://www.yelp.com/biz/mills-electric-brockton) per search snippet.

> **UNVERIFIED:** I could not read the individual reviewer names, dates or text off
> Google — Maps serves a JavaScript shell to automated fetching, and the profile returned
> no readable content. **Every name, year and quotation below is from CLAUDE.md, not
> from a page I read myself.** Years are almost certainly Google's approximate relative
> dates ("2 years ago") rather than exact ones. **Open the profile and read the four
> reviews before making any of these calls** — being wrong about what someone wrote is
> the one mistake that would make this backfire.

> Also note: **Mills' 4 Yelp reviews are probably not the same 4 people.** Nothing links
> them. Do not assume a name here also reviewed on Yelp, and do not add the counts.

# Priority 1 — Jose Oliveira

### THE SINGLE HIGHEST-VALUE ASK IN THIS DOCUMENT

| | |
|---|---|
| **Review** | 2024, 5★, **with text** |
| **Quoted in CLAUDE.md** | *"I've had the pleasure of relying on Mills Electric for over 8 years for commercial and residential jobs. They arrive promptly at the scheduled time, and are well-prepared with the necessary tools and equipment."* (truncated at source — the Google screenshot cut off) |
| **Asked for** | **A named commercial reference** — plus a referral. **NOT a review.** |
| **Channel** | Phone call. Not text, not email. |
| **Why first** | Eight-plus years, both commercial and residential, and he volunteered a detailed public review nobody asked him for. That is the profile of someone who will say yes. |

### Why he is not being asked for a review

He already wrote the best one on the profile. Asking again risks the delete-and-repost
problem above, and it would waste the far more valuable thing he can offer.

**What he can offer that nobody else on this list can: he is a commercial customer who
can vouch, by name, to another commercial buyer.** `COMPETITOR-REVIEW-GAP.md` shows the
Brockton market is won on volume Mills doesn't have yet — but commercial work isn't won
in the map pack. It's won when a property manager asks "who do you use?" One named
reference on a bid sheet is worth more revenue than ten residential reviews, and it is
available today for the price of a phone call.

### The call

> **"Jose, it's Charles Mills. You got a minute? Nothing's wrong — I'm calling for a
> favor, so just tell me if it's a bother."**
>
> *(let him answer)*
>
> **"First thing — I saw the review you left us on Google. I never said thank you for
> that and I should have. That meant a lot."**
>
> **"Here's why I'm calling. We're going after more of the commercial side — property
> managers, small facilities, that kind of work. When they ask for references, I'd like
> to be able to put you down. Someone who's used us eight, nine years on both the
> commercial and the residential end. Would you be alright with that — a name and a
> number on a reference sheet, maybe a phone call once in a while?"**
>
> *(if yes)*
>
> **"I appreciate it. I'll check with you before I give your number out to anybody — I'm
> not going to have people calling you out of the blue."**
>
> *(then, and only if the call is going well)*
>
> **"One more thing and then I'll let you go. If you know anybody else — another building,
> somebody in your line of work — who needs an electrician, I'd be glad of the
> introduction. We're a small shop. That's how most of our work comes."**

### Rules for this call

- **Thank him for the review first, and mean it.** If the ask comes before the thanks,
  the whole call reads as transactional.
- **"Tell me if it's a bother"** up front. Being openly direct that it's a favor is what
  makes a yes easy.
- **Promise to check before releasing his number — then actually do it.** A reference who
  gets ambushed stops being a reference.
- **Do not ask him for another review.** Not as an afterthought, not "while you're at it."
- If he offers to update his review unprompted — fine, let him, don't steer it.

### If he says yes, write it down

Name, company, title, phone, email, what work Mills did, what he's agreed to. That sheet
goes on commercial bids. **UNVERIFIED:** CLAUDE.md does not record which company Jose is
with or what the work was — the owner will know. Capture it on this call.

# Priority 2 — María "Loja"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Review** | 2025, 5★, **rating only — no text** |
| **Asked for** | **Two or three sentences added to the rating she already left.** |
| **Channel** | Text, or a short call if the owner knows her well. |
| **Why second** | The **highest-value review ask on this list**, and the shortest distance to travel. |

### Why this one is worth real effort

She is the most recent reviewer — 2025, per CLAUDE.md — so Mills is still fresh, and
**she has already done the hard part.** She found the profile, signed in, and clicked
five stars. The remaining step is typing a sentence. That is a far smaller ask than
anything in the 90-day plan involving a customer who has never reviewed anyone.

It also fixes a real weakness. A star rating with no words is nearly invisible to a
homeowner comparing contractors — people read reviews, they don't count them. **Two of
Mills' four reviews are silent.** Converting one into a sentence raises the quality of
the profile more than a fifth bare star would.

Google lets a reviewer **edit** an existing review and keep it. She does not need to
delete anything.

### The text

```
Hi Maria - Charles from Mills Electric. Thanks again for the 5 stars on
Google, I appreciate it. Small favor if you have a minute: would you add
a line or two about the work? Even one sentence helps people find us.
You can just edit the review you already left. [LINK] - Thanks, Charles
```

*(281 characters with a short link.)*

### The call, if the owner would rather phone

> **"María, it's Charles Mills, the electrician. You left us five stars on Google a while
> back and I wanted to say thank you — I don't think I ever did."**
>
> **"I've got a small favor. Would you mind adding a sentence or two to it? Just what we
> did and how it went. You don't have to write anything new — you can go back in and edit
> the one that's there. People read the words; the stars on their own don't tell them
> much."**
>
> **"Two sentences is plenty. I'll text you the link."**

### Cautions

- **"Edit the one you already left"** is the key phrase in both versions. Without it she
  may delete and start over, and if she gets interrupted halfway Mills is down to three
  reviews.
- **UNVERIFIED:** CLAUDE.md records her as `María "Loja"` — the quoted part is likely a
  Google display-name quirk rather than a surname. **Confirm how she is actually
  addressed before sending anything.** Getting a name wrong on the first line kills it.
- Ask once. No follow-up text.

> **"No, don't delete it — you can just edit the one that's there. And really, leave it
> as-is if that's easier. It's fine."**

| | |

| **Asked for** | **A sentence added to his rating** — or, more likely, **the work itself.** |
| **Channel** | Phone. |
| **Why last** | Six years cold. Same ask as María, far lower odds. |

### Be realistic about this one

2020 is a long time. He may have moved, changed his number, or have no memory of the job.
**The chance of converting this into review text is the lowest on the list** — which is
exactly why it is fourth, and why it should not be attempted until the first three are
done and the habit is running.

**But the call is still worth making, for a different reason.** A homeowner who was happy
enough to rate an electrician five stars in 2020 is a homeowner who has needed electrical
work since and called somebody else, because Mills never got back in touch. The realistic
return here isn't a review — **it's a job.**

### The call

> **"James? Charles Mills, Mills Electric. It's been a long time — we did some work for
> you back around 2020. Hope all's well."**
>
> **"Two reasons I'm calling. First, you left us five stars on Google back then and I
> never thanked you for it, so — thank you."**
>
> **"Second, I'm just checking in. If anything electrical has come up since — panel's
> getting old, outlets acting up, you've been thinking about a generator — give me a
> call before you call anybody else. We're still here, same number."**
>
> *(then, only if he's talkative and it's going well)*
>
> **"One last thing — if you ever get a minute, you could add a sentence to that review
> about what we did. No rush and no pressure at all."**

### Rules

- **Lead with the work, not the review.** The service check-in is the realistic value
  here; the review is a bonus.
- **Drop the review ask entirely if the call is lukewarm.** A cold 2020 customer who gets
  a favor request in the first thirty seconds hangs up.
- "Give me a call before you call anybody else" is the line that pays. A single panel
  upgrade is worth more than the entire 90-day review plan.
- If the number is dead, mark it and move on. Do not chase him across the internet.

## Rules that apply to all four

1. **Nobody has been contacted. Nothing here is scheduled.** These are drafts; the owner
   decides what gets said and to whom.
2. **Read the four reviews on the live profile first.** Everything above comes from
   CLAUDE.md, not from a page I could read. Confirm names, dates and wording.
3. **Thank them before asking.** Every one of these four did something unprompted and
   unthanked. Leading with the thanks is both decent and effective.
4. **Never ask an existing reviewer to delete and repost.** If they want to change it,
   they **edit** it. Say the word "edit" out loud.
5. **Never offer anything in exchange** — no discount, no free service call, no credit on
   the next job. This is the hardest rule to keep on a friendly call with a long-time
   customer and the easiest to break by accident. Google's policy prohibits incentivised
   reviews outright, and the penalty falls on the business, not the customer. See
   `REVIEW-ASK-KIT.md` §0.
6. **One ask each. No follow-ups on this list.** These are the best relationships Mills
   has. They are worth more than a review.
7. **Do not reply publicly to their reviews as a way of prompting them.** Replying to
   reviews is good practice and the owner should do it — but as a genuine thank-you,
   separately, not as a nudge. A public "hey, could you add more detail?" reads badly to
   every future customer who scrolls past it.
