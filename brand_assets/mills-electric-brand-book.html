01

At a Glance

Mills Electric is a licensed electrical contractor. Everything here should read as capable, safe, and squared‑away — a crew that shows up on time and leaves the panel cleaner than they found it.

We are
We are not
Direct, plain-spoken, confident
Salesy, jargon-heavy, gimmicky
Bold, structural, high-contrast
Soft, pastel, decorative, gradient-heavy
Industrial precision
Startup-playful or luxury-elegant
The three non-negotiables. Navy dominates. Yellow accents — it never dominates. Everything sits on a hard grid with square-ish corners. Target roughly 70% navy and neutrals, 25% white, 5% yellow. If a layout looks yellow-heavy, it is.

Logo

14 files · PNG

Two components: the mark (the interlocked ME monogram split by the bolt) and the wordmark. Together they form the primary lockup. The mark stands alone; the wordmark does not.

Variations

mills-primary-transparent.pngOver light photos or colored panels. Alpha channel preserved.
mills-mark-transparent.pngMark only. App icons, truck door, hard hat decal, watermark.

One-color production

Two-color is always preferred. Drop to mono only when the production method genuinely can’t do two — embroidery, single-plate print, laser etch.

mills-mono-navy.pngFaxes, one-color print, etched metal.
mills-mono-white.pngKnockout on photos, dark shirts, vinyl.
mills-mono-yellow.pngNavy shirts, safety vests, night-visible signage.

Clear space & minimum size

X = the cap-height of the M in the wordmark. Reserve X on all four sides. Nothing enters that zone — no text, no rules, no photo edges, no other logos.

zap is reserved. It echoes the logo bolt, so use it only for the company itself or top-level “electrical service” concepts — never as a generic bullet. Yellow icons are for emphasis only: a grid of twelve yellow icons is wrong. Make them navy and let one be yellow.

Photography over illustration

Real crews, real panels, real job sites. Prefer wide shots with strong horizontals and verticals — the brand is structural. Crop tight on hands-at-work and finished installs; avoid stock handshakes. Grade slightly cool to sit with the navy. For text legibility, overlay navy at 60–75% or run a navy-to-transparent gradient from the left. No stock illustration, no isometric vector scenes, no 3D renders.

Buttons

On navy surfaces, the button goes white.

48px tall (40 compact), 24px side padding, 6px radius, Inter 600. Focus ring is a 3px yellow halo on every variant. Primary is the yellow one, and there is exactly one per screen section.

The bolt accent

The logo’s bolt can inform a diagonal accent — an angled section divider or a sheared edge on a hero image. Three rules: the angle is always 12°, matching the bolt’s dominant slope; one diagonal per page; and never as a background texture, watermark, or repeating motif. It’s a signature, not a pattern.

07

Voice

Plain English. Lead with the outcome, then the credential. Read it out loud — if you run out of breath, cut it.

Write this
Not this
“We’ll replace your panel.”
“We facilitate electrical infrastructure modernization.”
“Same-day service in Kent County.”
“Fast, reliable service you can trust!”
“Lights back on today. Licensed and insured.”
“Don’t let faulty wiring burn your house down.”
Active voice, short sentences.
Passive constructions and stacked clauses.

Say licensed, bonded, insured exactly once per page — it’s a fact, not a slogan. No exclamation points in headlines. No fear-based safety copy: state the risk factually and offer the fix.

Naming. The legal name is Mills Electric Inc — no comma before Inc. On second reference, Mills Electric. Never “Mills Electrical” or “ME Inc” in customer-facing copy.
