What Bridgewater is built out of

Bridgewater is the newest of the eight towns on this list. Large subdivisions from the nineties and two-thousands make up much of it, alongside a historic centre and a substantial rental market around Bridgewater State University.

That changes what we get called for. A 1995 subdivision colonial already has a 200-amp service and a modern breaker panel. There is no knob-and-tube to replace and no fuse box to argue with the insurer about. The work here is additive — putting new load onto a system that can actually take it.

Bridgewater specifics

Adding load instead of chasing it

An EV charger in a Bridgewater subdivision is usually the straightforward version of the job: the service has spare capacity, the panel has slots, and the run to the garage is short. We still do the load calculation — a second charger, or a charger plus a heat pump, can take a 200-amp service closer to its limit than people expect — but the answer is normally yes, and the install passes first time.

Pool and hot tub circuits are the ones where the detail matters most. Equipotential bonding and the right GFCI protection are not optional and are exactly what an inspector will look at. We would rather do that properly than come back to it.

The rental market around the university brings a different list: working smoke and CO detectors in the right locations, and enough properly protected outlets that nobody in the house is running a space heater off a power strip. If you are a landlord here, that is a worthwhile annual visit.
